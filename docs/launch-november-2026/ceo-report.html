Executive assessment

Village Rewards has a defined proposition and published launch materials, but November launch readiness has not been demonstrated. The current source still uses older points logic and prototype access flows. Reward supply, operating funding, partner commitments and end-to-end production acceptance remain unverified.

Recommendation: keep November as a conditional pilot target. Release only when the core earn-and-redeem journey, real rewards, operating support and staff readiness are evidenced. A prize campaign can follow separately.

Evidence: current repository source and product decisions through 26 October 2026. No production database audit, signed partner agreements, customer adoption figures or confirmed funding were supplied for this report. These are evidence gaps, not claims that no work has occurred.

The commercial decision

Basic retailer participation is free. Listing, awarding village stamps and joining available village campaigns form the basic offer. The 90-day pilot does not trigger automatic billing. The previous A$30 monthly subscription proposal is withdrawn.

Seek association or sponsor support for running the shared service. Enhanced loyalty, promotion and reporting tools may become optional paid extras once demand and willingness to pay are demonstrated. No paid package or price is agreed. Everyday marketplace rewards remain voluntary retailer contributions.

This lowers the barrier to broad retailer participation, but leaves a funding decision to close. The program needs a budget for hosting, support, onboarding, maintenance and campaign administration; inexpensive software does not remove these responsibilities.

How the program will work

  1. A customer joins in their mobile browser and makes a qualifying purchase at a participating business.
  2. Authorised staff confirm the purchase. The customer earns one village stamp, regardless of spend.
  3. Stamps accumulate across businesses and can be spent on listed marketplace rewards. Only the reward cost is deducted.
  4. Retailers may voluntarily contribute offers or run separate promotions for their own customers.
  5. A proposed six-business purchase challenge may qualify customers for a sponsored draw once terms, funding and implementation are ready.

The ordinary stamp balance does not require visits to six shops. The challenge creates an additional reason to explore. Bigger spenders do not earn proportionally more under this visit-focused model.

Decisions required now

  • Operating owner and budget: nominate who runs the service, estimates costs and secures association or sponsor support.
  • Pilot scope and date: confirm the founding cohort and a launch date only after readiness evidence is available.
  • Reward supply: approve minimum catalogue coverage and stock, contributor responsibilities and shortage handling.
  • Customer rules: finalise qualifying purchases, split purchases, refunds, expiry and legacy point treatment.
  • Accountability: assign named leads for engineering, retailer onboarding, marketing, support and funding.
  • Optional extras: test merchant demand during the pilot before deciding features and pricing.

Next steps and launch gates

Next seven days: collect the trader and reward roster, prepare the operating budget, assign owners and close purchase policies. Engineering should provide a scoped implementation plan and acceptance evidence against the CTO report.

Before public enrolment: verify customer and trader permissions, one-stamp earning, duplicate prevention, stock-safe redemption, retained balances, migration and recovery. Complete a real-device rehearsal with trained staff and a working support channel.

Go/no-go: proceed with a limited pilot only when earning and fulfilment work reliably and adequate rewards and operational support are secured. If the prize is not ready, omit the draw. If core earning or reward supply is not ready, delay enrolment.

What success will mean

Agree targets before launch for active retailers, first-purchase conversion, repeat participation, customers buying at multiple shops, fulfilled rewards, reward availability and support workload. Review at days 30, 60 and 90.

Recorded transactions establish program use, not incremental sales. The pilot should establish whether customers find useful rewards, retailers value participation and the shared service can be sustainably funded.

The immediate priority is a funded, reliable pilot with useful rewards and participating businesses. Monetisation of optional tools comes after evidence of value.
